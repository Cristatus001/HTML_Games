The War College

Every cannonball obeys the same law: once it leaves the barrel, gravity owns it. The flight splits cleanly in two — sideways motion and vertical motion never interfere with each other.

Horizontal speed (vₓ) never changes — nothing is pushing the ball sideways once she's in the air.   vₓ = v₀ cos θ₀

Vertical speed (vᵧ) is a different story — gravity fights it the whole way up, stalls it dead at the peak, then hurls it back down. g = 9.8 m/s².   vᵧ = v₀ sin θ₀ − g t

The Peak — how high she climbs before gravity wins:   H = (v₀ sin θ₀)² ÷ (2g)

The Strike — how far she flies before she lands:   R = v₀² sin(2θ₀) ÷ g

The Gunner's Rule: at any given power, 45° sends the ball farthest on level ground. Every siege engineer since antiquity has known this.

Twin Shots: angles that add up to 90° (say, 30° and 60°) land in the exact same spot — one arcs high and lazy, the other screams in flat and fast. Choose your poison.

Old-world gunners learned these arcs by instinct and years of missed shots. This range ignores air resistance, so the numbers you see match the equations above exactly — no wind, no guesswork, no excuses.
