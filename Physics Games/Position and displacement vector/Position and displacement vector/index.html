How to Play

Objective: fly your airship from the blue pad to the gold pad. Your glowing trail is the distance you actually flew. The straight line between start and finish is your displacement. Score well by keeping them close.

  1. Fly by holding.
    Hold your finger (or the mouse button) anywhere on the sky and the ship flies to that spot. Move your finger and she follows it. Let go and she slows to a stop. On a keyboard use WASD or the Arrows instead.
  2. Land inside the gold ring.
    The dashed gold circle is the landing zone, and a small arrow on your ship always points at it. Just hold your finger over the ring and she will settle into it.
  3. Nothing here is punishing.
    You get three hulls, a moment of safety after every bump, and unlimited retries with R. Only spires and lit-up gates hurt you — clouds and islands are harmless.
  4. Watch your fuel.
    Fuel is measured in distance, not time. Hovering is free; wandering is not.
  5. Collect crystals where a mission asks for them, and use Space or the Beacon button to drop a survey beacon when one is offered.
  6. Read the reveal.
    On landing your trail is redrawn to show the distance, then a red laser fires to show the displacement. Three stars means you flew it tight.

R restarts a mission. There is no penalty for retrying.

Field Manual

Position vector (r)

An arrow from a chosen origin to an object. It answers "where is it?" — but only relative to a reference point somebody picked.

  • Written r = (x, y), measured in units from the origin.
  • Left and down are negative, so (-3, -2) is three left and two down.
  • Move the origin and every position vector changes, even though nothing physically moved. That is what the survey beacons in Mission 2 and Mission 8 show you.

Displacement (Δr)

The straight-line change in position from start to finish — a vector, so it has both a size and a direction.

Δr = r_final − r_initial

Because the origin cancels out in that subtraction, displacement is identical in every frame of reference. That is why the two beacons in Mission 8 disagree about your position but agree exactly about your displacement.

Distance (d) versus displacement

Distance is a scalar: the total length of the route you flew, every wobble included. It is the number climbing in your HUD and the trail glowing behind you.

Displacement is a vector and only cares about the two endpoints. So:

  • Fly a detour for crystals: distance grows, displacement does not move at all.
  • Fly a perfect straight line: d = |Δr| — the only case where they agree.
  • Fly a round trip home: distance is large, displacement is zero (Mission 7).
  • In general d ≥ |Δr|, always. Never less.

Your efficiency score is simply |Δr| ÷ d as a percentage.

Adding vectors

In Mission 6 a crosswind is added to your thrust every frame. Where you actually go is the sum of the two arrows, not the one you pointed. Vectors add tip-to-tail, component by component: (a₁+b₁, a₂+b₂).
