How to Play

  • 1

    Objective — find the equal vectors. Each case shows two arrows taken from a real-life situation. Decide whether they are the same vector.

  • Drag the amber arrow by its round tail node and drop it on the reference node — the ringed target at the tail of the grey arrow. Touch and mouse both work.

  • Once the tails snap together the Vector Scanner runs automatically and checks two things: magnitude (size/speed) and direction (angle).

  • If both match, the vectors are equal — the arrow lights up and colour rain falls. If either fails, the scanner explains exactly which property differs.

  • Use Next Case to advance or Retry to reset the current case. Progress is saved automatically on this device.

The Textbook Definition

In Physics and Mathematics, two vectors A and B are EQUAL if and only if:

1. |A| = |B|  (Same Magnitude)
2. Direction of A = Direction of B

Crucial note: the initial point (starting position) does not matter. A vector can be moved anywhere parallel to itself without changing.

Significance & Applications

Why is this significant? Vectors let us model the world consistently, no matter where an event happens.

1. Force calculationEngineers slide force vectors along beams to simplify bridge math.
2. Field theoryMagnetic and wind fields rely on the concept of uniform equal vectors across space.
3. Game physicsA "jump" vector works the same way whether the player is at the start or end of the level.
4. CollisionsRelative velocity vectors predict crashes regardless of map coordinates.

Case Notes

Every sample in this study came from an ordinary scene. Rain in two different yards, two lifts on different floors, two lanes of traffic — all produced equal vectors, because only length and heading count.

The failures were just as instructive: opposite escalators reverse direction, a marching band turn changes the angle, and a fast car versus a slow car changes magnitude alone.
