How to Play

Objective: You are a field expedition team registering species in the Global Registry. Complete five survey sites by writing each scientific name correctly.

Controls

  • Build the Name: tap a word to pick it up, then tap a slot to place it. Tap a filled slot to send the word back.
  • Multiple choice: tap the card you believe is correct.
  • Field notebook: tap each word to toggle its underline.
  • Register species: type the full name, then tap the register button. On a computer you can also press Enter.

Everything works with touch, mouse, or keyboard. Earn discovery points for each correct answer and advance to the next site.

Reference & background — scroll for more

Binomial nomenclature is the two-part naming system introduced by Carolus Linnaeus. Every species gets a Genus name and a species epithet.

The rules:

  • The Genus is capitalised: Mangifera.
  • The species epithet is lowercase: indica.
  • In print, the whole name is italicised: Mangifera indica.
  • When handwritten, each word is underlined separately instead of italicised.
  • The author citation (the person who first described it) may follow, e.g. Mangifera indica Linn. — "Linn." abbreviates Linnaeus.
  • Only two words form the name (Genus + species); keep proper spacing between them.

Examples: Homo sapiens Linn. (humans), Musca domestica Linn. (housefly), Mangifera indica Linn. (mango), Triticum aestivum Linn. (wheat), Solanum melongena Linn. (brinjal).

Author citations: the correct abbreviation for Linnaeus is Linn. Watch out for wrong or misleading citations such as Roxb. (Roxburgh), Lam. (Lamarck), or an author wrapped in brackets like (Linn.), which instead signals a later reclassification.

Lore: Naming new species from the field lets scientists worldwide refer to the same organism without confusion — a shared language for life on Earth.
