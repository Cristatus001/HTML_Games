Field Journal

Reference notes on the economic importance of algae.

What algae are

Algae are simple, mostly aquatic photosynthetic organisms. They range from single-celled diatoms and Chlorella to giant kelps many metres long, and they have no true roots, stems or leaves.

Economic uses

    Not made from algae

    The morsels that make Finn shrink are everyday manufactured goods with no algal origin — useful reminders of where the boundary sits.

      Quick glossary

      Primary producer
      An organism that makes its own food by photosynthesis and forms the base of a food chain.
      Hydrocolloid
      A gel-forming substance such as agar, alginate or carrageenan, extracted from algae.
      Biofertiliser
      A living or biological soil additive that raises fertility — here, nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.
      Phycoremediation
      Using algae to strip pollutants and heavy metals out of water.
